Rooms and encores

What every exercise carries, how honesty shows on the page, and why each lesson ends on a game.

Every exercise lives in one of the learner's two rooms and ends with something you can point at. The lesson ends with a game, played rather than prepared.

What an exercise carries

  • a room
  • honest minutes
  • numbered steps
  • a success you can observe

The room is the learner's own frame, named by them at intake. In this demo the rooms are Draft, which ends with something written, and Try, which ends with something seen. The runtime tags each exercise and adds a done box; ticking every box in a lesson lights its tile on the board.

Honesty on the page

Every operational claim links its source. A claim that no source confirms keeps a visible flag, like this one: example flag: check it yourself. The check tool counts flags per page and fails when one disappears without a recorded reason.

Reminders live in dotted words, like encore. They remind an expert; they never teach the basics.

Write the success first

  1. Take an exercise you would give a learner. Write only its success condition, as something a person could see or hear.
  2. Now write the steps that lead there, and cut any step the success condition does not need.

the success line names something observable, and every step serves it.

Two-minute lesson

  1. Start the timer. In two minutes, sketch one lesson on paper: its idea, one figure, one game.
  2. When time runs out, stop mid-line. Next round, a new subject. Three rounds.

three sketches, each with one idea, one figure and one game, and one you would actually build.

Before you build a course

  • You write the success condition before the steps.
  • You flag what no source confirms, and leave the flag.
  • You end every lesson on something played.
